Designed around a working life

Make room for the rest of your day.

The idea is to make a working day feel a little more personal. The game’s rewards have limits; they are never your wages.

≈

A shift gives a fixed number of nets.

The shift-completion reward does not increase with the length of that shift.

◷

Game coins stop when your cat tires.

Coins accumulate during shifts, with a daily cap and a 7.5-hour daily limit. They cannot be exchanged for money.

✦

Your island is there on days off.

Collect, decorate and dress up without needing another shift. Rest is part of the loop, alongside making your space your own.

A closer look, for judges

Here’s what you can evaluate today.

A playable game, a proposed business, and a hypothesis to test. Each has a different level of evidence.

Here’s what you can evaluate today.
The game
Playable prototype		Companion quiz, sample job cards, working together, orb collecting, wardrobe and island building.

The film shows these screens; the browser build lets you explore them.

The jobs
Sample data		Listings and demo shifts are examples, not live job offers.

The current prototype does not establish real hiring outcomes.

The business
Proposed model		A free core game, with employers paying for listings and shift matching.

Optional cosmetic purchases are planned. Payment flows and employer willingness to pay still need validation.

The impact
To validate		Do workers choose to return between shifts, and does the companion feel supportive?

A worker pilot is the next step. Effects on sleep, overwork and hiring remain unvalidated.

The game

Godot 4, with 3D characters and a browser export.

The platform

A TypeScript API and a separate employer web app in the monorepo.

The data

Local game progress plus a separate usage-analytics path. See the data notes for its boundaries.
